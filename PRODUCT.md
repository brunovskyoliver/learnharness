# Brunovský's study

A learning application for guided lessons, practice, and a persistent knowledge graph.

The learner reads explanations, experiments with demos, and answers questions. The interface must keep the relevant explanation, visualization, and answer accessible together. Lessons and practice retain their existing question mechanics and saved history.

Primary surfaces: home and knowledge graph, chains, new lesson/practice, session transcript and questions, demo workspace, page pane, Library, background activity, settings and setup. Hosted authentication and the Electron shell remain supported. Home leads with "Waiting for you": every unanswered check and open problem, then every lesson whose plan still has unchecked topics, with a Continue that drafts the next topic without sending it (features 002–003). A "Calibration" section shows how stated confidence matched graded results, with the checks answered wrong while Certain (feature 004).

Teacher and practice agents can check computations with Wolfram through a fixed MCP connection (feature 007). Practice accepts a correct answer written in a different form, and a quiz answer checked before it was posed is labelled "Checked with Wolfram" once answered.

Workspace navigation (features 014-015) connects these surfaces: the sidebar lists lessons, practice and the Library's folders, tabs keep open sessions, notes and documents one step away across reloads, the right pane can collapse or show an outline and context for the current item, and Cmd/Ctrl+K opens a command palette over sessions, the Library and actions. Drafts, reading positions and the live demo stay with their existing owners, and nothing here calls a model.

Local review examples are available through `npm run dev:review`. They cover lesson/demo exploration, a knowledge check, active practice and practice history. Existing data and edits are preserved; the examples are optional and do not seed hosted tenants. See [docs/ui-review.md](docs/ui-review.md).

Both modes share one paper design (feature 025, 2026-11-07): Notebook's warm paper and grain, light or dark or following the system, Instrument Sans for the interface and the learner's words, the tutor's prose in Newsreader in the chosen AI ink, ink-filled primary actions and one warm bulb colour for "Your turn". The same ☰ and wordmark, overlay sidebar, corner hint, menus, dialogs and settings modal appear in Study and Notebook, so switching modes changes the content, not the look. Customize sets theme, text size, lettering and AI ink once for both; older reading preferences stay saved but no longer style the app. A lesson check or practice problem opens as a thread beside the paragraph it follows (inline on narrow screens), and the bottom bar keeps free chat with direct model, reasoning and usage controls. Scrollbars stay hidden while scrolling remains functional. Legacy arbitrary HTML remains sandboxed and supports whole-demo expansion; individual graph focus requires the demo kit or an explicit adapter. Theme changes reach the live demo without reloading it, so its parameters, simulation history and camera survive.

The Library (feature 009) holds the learner's documents and pages, in hosted and desktop versions, and replaces Settings → Files. Uploads never overwrite and deletes confirm. Typing @ in any composer or answer field references an item; the agent receives it in full or as an outline, and a visible label says which. Pages are editable Markdown and math documents beside the chat with restorable versions. The learner writes them, the agent can create them, and every AI edit to an existing page waits for the learner to accept it. Agent page tools can be turned off in Settings → Agent. An item's content is treated as material, not as instructions. Nested folders organise pages and uploads together (feature 011); moving something changes only where it shows, never its bytes, history or references. Items can be tagged, favorited, pinned and archived; deleting moves things to a recoverable trash, which keeps them until the learner deletes them for good (feature 012). Sort, filter, grid and list views, bulk actions, right-click menus and drag and drop arrive with feature 013.

Flashcards (feature 021) keep what was learned within reach. A card is written by hand or drafted from a note, a PDF passage or a lesson on an explicit click, and the learner reads each draft before keeping it. `/review` shows the day's due cards under FSRS scheduling: the front, then on request the back and four ratings with predicted gaps, by keyboard or touch, with undo, history, suspend and recoverable delete. Cards link to their source and to knowledge-graph ideas, and the ones tied to a weak idea can come first. Scheduling and understanding stay apart: a review never counts as evidence, and evidence never moves a schedule. Home shows how many are due.

Notes connect (features 016-018). Pages link to each other with `[[Page name]]`, which stays plain Markdown and keeps its meaning through a rename; a dangling link says so and is never lost. Each page lists what links to it, what it links to, related notes, the lessons it came from and the knowledge-graph ideas it touches, all worked out from what is stored with no model call. Callouts and collapsible sections keep a Markdown form any viewer can read. Selected lesson text can be saved to a page with a link back to that message, and a lesson can become a page of study notes with one click: a plain extract of its own prose. The graph is only read, so notes never count as evidence. On selected page text the tutor can also summarize, rewrite, expand, challenge and ask graded questions. Simpler, Rewrite and Expand are held apart from the page (feature 023): he reads what would change, then accepts, rejects or edits it first, and nothing moves until he accepts. A page that moved on refuses, with nothing lost. Other changes the tutor makes in a turn are restorable versions, shown for review with Undo, and his draft is always saved first.

Documents are searchable and readable in place (features 019-020). The Library's field also searches inside pages, text files and PDFs, with snippets, filters and a jump to the passage, through a deterministic index kept apart from the files. A scan, an image or an unreadable file says so by name; nothing is silently left out. PDFs open beside the lesson with selection, highlights and notes that never touch the file. A question on a passage goes to the tutor only on a click, labelled, and page citations open the page, mark the quote and say honestly when it isn't there. Passages can be selected by keyboard too, reaching the same actions (feature 023).

Diagrams, concept maps and whiteboards live in the Library beside pages (feature 022), with autosave, undo, History and SVG, PNG and JSON export, editable by pointer, touch or keyboard, with a list form on narrow screens. Nodes link to notes, lessons and graph ideas; linking only reads the graph. On request the tutor can propose a diagram or a change: the learner sees what would change, and nothing happens until he approves.

The ML and LLM lab (feature 008) adds inline and composed panels for real token splits, illustrative attention/sampling/embeddings, computed small networks and tensor shapes. Teacher and researcher agents can look up public Hugging Face configs and papers, search Semantic Scholar with arXiv fallback, and cite up to ten paper pages per turn. Anonymous source lookups are capped at five Hub calls and five paper searches per turn; only queries and identifiers leave the server. ML visuals, Model hub and Papers are independently switchable in Settings → Agent. Token edits stay local to the tokenizer route and never call a model.

Notebook mode (feature 024) is a second way to write: one quiet page, after Riddled's notebook, chosen from either sidebar, the palette or Mod+Shift+O, with Study one step away. The learner writes a Library page; a model reads along when he pauses ("Thinks with you") or only when he asks ("When you ask"), and answers with a short question in the margin, in its own lettering, pointed at a specific claim. He can open any note into a thread and write back, resolve or dismiss it, select words to think about, ask about or visualize as a drawing, chat with the whole note, set an instruction and a reviewer, hide every AI layer with Just my words, and export to PDF, Word or Markdown with or without the notes. The AI never edits his text. Every automatic read is shown with its cost in the corner and can be stopped; notes and threads stay with the page, not with sessions.
